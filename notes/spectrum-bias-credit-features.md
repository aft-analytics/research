# Spectrum bias and credit features

In medicine this issue is called spectrum bias (sometimes spectrum effect): a diagnostic test built on a very sick population often fails when applied to a general population. Unless the study sample covers an appropriately broad spectrum of diseased and nondiseased patients, sensitivity and specificity can look much better than they will in practice. [[1]](#1)

By analogy, in a financially struggling population it may matter how much unused credit limit a person has. In the general population, people may not rely on their remaining credit-card line for survival. Features that represent “struggle” can therefore be misinterpreted—another example being inquiries (desperation in a high-cost / RM-style book versus shopping around for other people).

The reverse also holds. Softer signals that work in the general population—more history, more products—are weak or absent for a specialty lender, because the people who generate those signals are largely outside the book.
